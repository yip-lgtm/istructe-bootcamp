# 📚 PART 1: Foundation Scheme (p82-83)

## 1.1 Pad / Strip / Raft Footing on Rock/Soil

**When to use:** Strong shallow soil + small column loads + economy choice

**Load Path:**
```
Column → Pad Footing → Rock (end bearing + friction key)
Lateral: Rock → Shear Key; Soil → Dmin & Base friction
```

**Feasibility Calculation Example:**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Allowable Column Load | 57,000 kN |
| Qa (bearing pressure) | 5,000 kPa |
| Bearing Area req'd | 11.4 m² |
| Provided | 4.5m × 4.5m pad = 16 m² |
| Result | OK |

**Stability Checks (Part 1(b)):** Overturning, Sliding, Uplifting

**Detailed Design Checks:** Settlement, Differential settlement, Angular rotation

🔥 **Distinction Tip:** Show calculation + reference to Code of Practice

---

## 1.2 Socketed Steel H-Pile

**When to use:** Weak shallow soil + building size + column loads + rock available

**Load Path:**
```
Column → Pile Cap → Socket Steel-H Pile → Rock (end bearing + shaft friction)
```

**Feasibility Calculation Example:**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Allowable Column Load | 21,600 kN |
| Pile Type | UC 305×305×223 kg/m S450J0 UBP |
| Effective Rock Socket | 6.8 m |
| Rock-grout bond | 2,618 kN |
| Steel-grout bond | 6,169 kN |
| **Pile capacity** | **6,106 kN** |
| Piles per cap | 4 nos. of 610mm Ø pile |

**Detailed Design Checks:** Lateral load, Deflection, Combined axial + bending

**Law Court Q1 Application:**

| Site Condition | Recommended Foundation |
|----------------|------------------------|
| Strong shallow soil + small column load | Pad/Strip/Raft |
| Weak shallow soil + rock not deep | Socket H-pile |
| Very weak soil + high column load | Large Diameter Bored Pile (p81) |

🔥 **Distinction Tip:** Right-sized foundation = less embodied carbon

# 📚 PART 5: 2024 Sustainability Update (p96-97)

## 5.1 Reduce Material Use Strategies (p96)

**Logical thinking path** when client wants to reduce material/carbon/cost:

1. Relocate mechanical plant to basement (avoid heavy loads at upper levels)
2. Reduce floor-to-floor heights (integrate MEP with structural zone)
3. Relocate cores for greater efficiency
4. Relax column spacing restrictions
5. Allow bracing in external façade
6. Remove / reduce cantilevered section
7. Remove basement (if feasible)
8. Reduce imposed loads
9. Add beams in no-floor zone (reduce column + mullion size)

---

## 5.2 Case 1 — Strategic Columns to Reduce Transfer (p97)

**Current Issue:** Levels 1-3 must be column-free; Level 4 transfer structure: beams 16-24m, depth 1,500-2,200mm; 35-40% of total structural material

**Proposed Change:** Add 2-4 slender columns (300-400mm dia) in column-free zone, aligned with Level 4+ grid

**Benefits:**
- Eliminates transfer structure (2,200mm → 600mm beams)
- Reduces transfer span by 50% (16-24m → 8-12m)
- Foundation sizes reduced 30-40%
- Columns can be architectural features
- Maintains 12-16m flexible layouts

---

## 5.3 Case 2 — Differential Floor Heights (p97)

**Current:** 2.70m clear + 350mm services = 3.40m floor-to-floor (uniform)

**Proposed (Differential):**

| Level | Clear | Services | F2F |
|-------|-------|----------|-----|
| 1-3 (public/conference) | 2.70m | 250mm (integrated) | 3.20m |
| 4-7 (offices) | 2.60m | 250mm (integrated) | 3.10m |

**Total Saving: 400mm across 4 floors**

**Benefits:** 8-10% wind load reduction, vertical elements saving, PT flat slabs, office 2.60m imperceptible

# 📚 PART 6: Case 3 + 4 Examples (p98)

## 6.1 Case 3 — Strategic Shear Walls at Perimeter

**Current Issue:** "No shear walls except around cores" → forces moment frame (20-30% material penalty)

**Proposed Change:** 
- Option A (preferred): Two 6-8m walls at N/S ends
- Option B: Four 3-4m corner walls
- Wall thickness: 250-300mm, integrated within facade

**Benefits:**
- Eliminates moment frame → pinned connections (50-60% less rebar)
- Column size: 600×600 → 450×450 (44% concrete saving)
- Beam size: 600×800 → 500×650 (28% concrete saving)
- Foundation moments reduced 60% → 20-25% smaller foundations
- Enhanced lateral stiffness, reduced drift
- Accommodates windows (coupled wall design)

---

## 6.2 Case 4 — Rationalize Plant Room Column Arrangement

**Current Issue:** Plant room Level 4: max 6 columns, min 9.0m spacing; long-span roof (1,200mm deep beams), large columns (500×500mm)

**Proposed Change:** Increase columns 6 → 10-12 nos., reduce spacing to 6-7m grid

**Benefits:**
- Roof beam spans 9m → 6-7m (22-33% reduction)
- Roof beam depth 1,200mm → 700mm (42% reduction)
- Column size 500×500 → 400×400 (36% concrete saving)
- Lighter roof → reduced loads below

## 7.2 Solutions Section (p100)

**Solution 1:** Increase the section size of the primary roof beams and primary columns to achieve the longer span and taller height. Similarly, increase the section size of the bracing members as required to resist the additional lateral loads.

**Solution 2:** Reduce the grid spacing between primary beams and columns, thereby reducing the load on each member. This will result in an increased number of structural members. The design of the foundations will also be impacted, with additional foundations required and increased size of foundations to support the increased load.

### Please Be Aware That the Proposed Change Could Result In:

**Construction Implications:**
- Additional temporary works and coordination required
- Possible need for additional craneage or temporary propping

**Cost Implications:**
- A. The proposal will increase the overall cost of the project by approximately _ % due to additional structural elements and temporary works (confirmed by QS)
- B. Additional cost due to increased number/size of structural members

**Time Implications:**
- Increase in construction time → loss of revenue
- Time delay due to re-submission to local authority

# 📚 PART 8: 2019 Jul Q4 Letter Example (p102-103)

## 8.1 Topic: Add Swimming Pool to Podium

**Opening:** Further to our recent meeting regarding your enquiry about adding a swimming pool to the external podium area, please note we have reviewed the issue and our response is noted below:

**Main Body:**

- **Location:** Best location is between gridlines 1–5 and D–H, away from the transfer structure supporting the guestroom towers.

- **Height Analysis:**
  - 5.2m floor-to-floor (L1 to L2)
  - Required: 3.0m clear + 0.7m building services + drainage slope (1:100)
  - **Solution:** Raise pool deck 1,300mm above typical
  - Result: 1,800mm deep pool accommodated

- **Structural Impact:**
  - Increased weight from water → increase sizes of slabs, beams, columns and foundations under pool area

- **Waterproofing (Critical):**
  - Risk of leakage to basement carpark
  - **Solution:** RC shell + internal waterproofing membrane + internal shotcrete layer + waterproofing admixtures

- **Other Coordination:**
  - Architectural redesign of podium roof (landscaping, shade structures, hardscaping)
  - New plant room (pumps, filters)
  - Basement dropped area modification (building services)

- **Overall Assessment:**
  - Not a major change, but significant impact on completed podium roof design
  - Will discuss with design team: local authority approval, construction schedule, cost estimate

**Closing:** Please let us know whether you wish to proceed with the proposed changes. We trust that we have informed you sufficiently.

🔥 **Distinction Features:** Bullet points, each with 問題 + 解決方案 + 影響, specific data (1,300mm raise, 1,800mm pool depth), risk mitigation, coordination considerations, final overall assessment

# 📚 PART 9: 2025 Jul Q2 Letter Example (p104-106) ⭐

## 9.1 Topic: 4 Material Reduction Proposals

**Opening:** Comprehensive review, 4 key proposals to reduce material while maintaining gross floor area.

### PROPOSAL 1: PERMIT STRATEGIC INTERNAL COLUMNS AT LEVELS 1-3

**Current Constraint & Impact:** Client requires "no internal columns in Levels 1-3 (except cores)" → Level 4 has massive transfer structure (beams up to 30m, depth 1,800-2,500mm)

**Proposed Change:** 6 strategic internal columns at Levels 1-3, 12m × 14m grid, aligned with Level 4-5 columns

**Design Impact:**

*Structural Benefits:*
- Eliminates transfer structure at L4
- Transfer beam depth 2,500mm → 600mm typical
- Standard beam sections (600×900mm) throughout
- Foundation sizes reduced 35%

*Layout Implications:*
- L1 Retail: 6 retail units of ~168m² each (aligns with industry 150-200m²)
- L2-3 Office: 12m × 14m grid, open-plan flexibility, co-located with MEP risers

**Recommendation:** Highest material saving potential. Cost/carbon savings (£245k, 647 tonnes CO₂) substantially outweigh minor layout constraints.

---

### PROPOSAL 2: OPTIMIZE FLOOR-TO-FLOOR HEIGHTS

**Current:** 3.0m clear + 900mm services = 3.9m floor-to-floor (conservative)

**Proposed:** 3.5m floor-to-floor (clear 2.7m + structural 500mm PT slabs + services 300mm integrated)

**Benefits:**
- Building height reduction: 1.6m total
- Vertical elements saving: 425 tonnes concrete
- Reduced wind loads: 8% lower height = 15% lower wind moment
- Cladding reduction: 850m²

---

### PROPOSAL 3: INTRODUCE PERIMETER STABILITY SYSTEM

**Current:** Only cores for stability → moment frame (25-35% material penalty)

**Proposed:** Discrete steel cross-bracing in 4 corner bays (6m wide), hidden in 200mm precast cladding

**Benefits:**
- Eliminates moment frame → pinned connections (60% less rebar)
- Column size 600×600 → 450×450 (44% less concrete)
- Beam size 600×900 → 500×750 (31% less concrete)
- Foundation moments reduced 65% → 25% smaller foundations
- Zero visual impact

**Recommendation:** Exceptional material efficiency with zero architectural compromise.

---

### CONCLUSION (p106)

> These four proposals offer substantial material reduction, carbon savings and cost reduction while maintaining the required floor area. The changes align with modern commercial design standards and enhance long-term building value through improved adaptability and circular economy principles. We recommend proceeding with the full package to maximize sustainability performance and position the project as a benchmark for low-carbon city centre development. We would welcome the opportunity to discuss these proposals in detail and address any questions you may have.

# 📚 PART 10: CONSIDERATION Table (p107)

## 10.1 Three Categories of Impact

### Structures / Design
- **Increase / added loads:** Increase columns, foundations, reinforcement; increase column/footing size; possible differential settlement
- **Increase floor area:** Increase concrete volume, column + foundation number; E.J. / M.J. may be required; structural efficiency may improve
- **Increase number of storey:** Building height increases → wind load critical; increase column + foundation loading; aspect ratio change may affect stability

### Construction
- Ease of construction
- Need skilled labour or specialist contractors?
- Programme changes (delay? re-submission?)
- Additional equipment and tools?

### Cost
- Extension of construction period → overhead cost
- Material cost (concrete, steel, curtain walling)

# 📚 PART 12: Other Cases + Add Basement Example (p116-118)

## 12.1 Raising Building Level (p116)

**Two possible methods:**

### Method 1: Increase raft footing thickness (+500mm)
- Dead load increase: ~+12 kPa
- Causes overburden on geotextile membrane
- Not recommended

### Method 2: Suspended slab above raft (150mm thk)
- Dead load increase: ~+3.6 kPa
- Supported by bearing walls
- More feasible

**Other impacts:**
- Raft size needs to increase (28m×47m → 30m×49m)
- Building height + wind load + vertical elements + roof steel frame all slightly increase
- Redesign: ~3 weeks + 1 month statutory amendment (minor)
